Shares are typical planning guides, not quotes. Your venue and caterer will always have the final say.

How to use this wedding budget calculator: start with the most you can comfortably spend, then your guest count — catering is worked out per guest because food and drink are usually the biggest per-head cost. The category amounts are pre-filled with the shares couples typically spend (venue around 20%, photography and video around 12%), so you can see straight away whether your quotes are in line. In the US, full-service catering commonly runs $70–$100 per guest, and around £55–£80 in the UK, though a relaxed buffet or food truck can bring that right down. Edit any category to your real quotes as they come in; if the summary turns red, you are over budget and it is time to trim the flexible items — flowers, décor and the buffer — before touching the things your guests will actually remember.

How much alcohol do you need for a wedding? The rule of thumb bartenders use is one drink per drinking guest for every hour of the party — guests tend to have two drinks in the first hour and roughly one an hour after that, so this average works out about right. This calculator splits those drinks between wine, beer and spirits using the sliders: a standard 750ml wine bottle pours five glasses, a 750ml spirits bottle makes about sixteen mixed drinks, and beer is simply one bottle or can per drink. Champagne is counted separately — one toast glass for every guest, drinker or not, at six glasses per bottle. If your crowd are light drinkers, drop the "guests who drink" figure to 60–70%; if it is a late-night party crowd, keep it at 80–90% and add a bottle or two of each as a safety margin — unopened bottles can usually be returned or kept for the next celebration.
